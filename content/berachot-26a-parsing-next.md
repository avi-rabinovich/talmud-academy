# Parsing quiz 2: after midday and a missed Minchah

Status: editorial draft for educator review. Audience: first-time Gemara learners around age 11.

Source: [Bavli Berachot 26a](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.26a), from וכולי עלמא עד חצות through ואין בזה משום דעבר יומו בטל קרבנו. Printed abbreviations follow the displayed scan.

Four selections, worth 10 points each:

1. **Question:** וכ״ע עד חצות … שחרית מתפלל במנחה שתים. Include the cited statement about making up a missed prayer; והאמר is a challenge, not the answer.
2. **Answer:** כולי יומא מצלי ואזיל … שכר תפלה בזמנה לא יהבי ליה. Include both sides of the distinction between reward for prayer and reward for prayer at its proper time.
3. **Question:** איבעיא להו … כל אימת דבעי מצלי ואזיל. Include the initial question and both possible lines of reasoning. את״ל and או דילמא are inside the question, not its resolution.
4. **Answer:** ת״ש דאמר … ואין בזה משום דעבר יומו בטל קרבנו. Include the cited ruling and its explanation. Stop before מיתיבי, which begins the next objection.

Explain after the student locks each selection. The second exchange asks whether mistakenly missing Minchah can be made up at Maariv, considering the change of day and the nature of prayer as a request for mercy. The response cites a teaching permitting the makeup prayer. This is an explanation of the passage, not a stand-alone practical prayer guide.

The final five selectable words begin the following objection so students can overshoot the last answer. The quiz checks reading boundaries, not spoken translation. Data and image coordinates live in `parsing-next.js`.
